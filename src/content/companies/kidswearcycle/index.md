KidswearCycle is a responsive resale website concept helping parents buy and sell children's clothing. The aim is to make affordable second-hand clothing easier to find and give outgrown items a longer useful life.

The project explores browsing, item details and checkout across desktop, tablet and mobile, with an emphasis on clear categories and a straightforward purchase journey.

## Understanding the User

Early exploration used AI-generated personas and simulated interviews to develop assumptions about parents' needs. This provided hypotheses for the design; it was not research with real participants. A separate moderated usability study later tested the low-fidelity prototype with four participants.

### Initial design hypotheses

- **Time:** make browsing and purchasing convenient for busy parents.
- **Affordability:** help people find suitable second-hand clothing within a budget.
- **Navigation:** use clear categories and filters to reduce search effort.
- **Accessibility:** consider visual hierarchy, contrast and navigation cues.

## Usability Testing & Findings

A moderated usability study tested the low-fidelity prototype with four participants. Sessions lasted 15-20 minutes.

Three issues guided the next design iteration:

- **Category selection:** the category block on a category page was confusing.
- **Item details:** seller reviews occupied too much space compared with the item description.
- **Checkout:** delivery options needed a clearer presentation.

### Accessibility considerations

The design uses heading sizes to establish hierarchy and icons to support navigation. Colour combinations were checked with WebAIM Contrast Checker; this does not replace testing the implemented site with assistive technologies.

## Learnings & Next Steps

The study highlighted the importance of balancing item information, seller information and checkout choices. Small changes to these areas can improve the clarity of the purchase journey.

Further work would explore buyer-seller messaging and user profiles, refine search filters and test the updated design with participants. The initial AI-generated assumptions also need validation through direct research with parents.
